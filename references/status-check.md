# Status check · the truth gate that runs with the voice gate

The voice rules defend *how* a text sounds. This file defends *whether it is true about what it claims to be*. A sentence can have a flawless source and still lie about what kind of claim it is. Both gates run together; no text closes on one of them. Language-neutral: examples are given in English with Portuguese where the pattern differs.

## The mechanics (three ways truth erodes)

1. **The caveat evaporates (reification).** Every retelling compresses, and the first thing cut is the status marker ("think of it as", "for teaching purposes", "works like"), because it carries the least information per word. The metaphor survives; the frame that said what to do with it does not. Classical names: misplaced concreteness (Whitehead, 1925), category mistake (Ryle, 1949), absolute metaphor (Blumenberg).
2. **The question contaminates (sycophancy).** Models tend to agree with the premise built into the question. "Why is X a Y?" produces a competent defence of a false premise, not a refusal. Contamination happens before any hallucination, and no source audit catches it, because every cited source may be correct.
3. **The average wins (textbook consensus).** Models return what was written most, not what is most true. The textbook summary outweighs the critical edition in any corpus, so the error is always the same error: the simplification that circulates most.

This is not new with AI: textbooks spread the "thesis-antithesis-synthesis" formula as Hegel's for a century, though it is Fichte's terminology, popularised by Chalybäus in 1837. Machines accelerate the mechanism; they did not invent it.

## Three dimensions of what gets lost

| Dim. | What it is | How it is lost | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 · Status** | what kind of claim: definition, analogy, model, hypothesis | the negative analogy disappears and the image becomes a definition | "the brain is a computer" |
| **D2 · Provenance** | where it came from, who holds it | the origin is lost by repetition or swapped for a more prestigious one | "100 billion neurons", with no locatable primary source for decades |
| **D3 · Uncertainty** | the margin, and whether the field agrees | the interval becomes a point; a dispute becomes apparent consensus | the corrected 86 billion circulating as an exact figure |

A flawless source covers only D2. That is why source checking alone is not enough.

## Five status questions (run on every central claim)

1. **Being or process?** Does the sentence say *is / consists of / means* about something the source treats as a process, disposition or operation? Put the process verb back.
2. **Is the metaphor working in secret?** Remove the image: is the claim still true? If not, declare its status or reword it.
3. **Where is this written?** Can it be anchored in a primary text with an exact location (work, chapter, section, edition, page), or does it come from a tertiary summary?
4. **Who disagrees?** If there is documented disagreement and the text presents one reading as the only one, it lies by omission. Map the dispute.
5. **Where did this sentence come from?** If a model generated it and nobody checked it against a source, it does not exist yet: it is a hypothesis waiting for verification.

## Seven erosion patterns (where to look on purpose)

1. **Caveat pruning:** the qualification vanished, the content stayed (a small effect became a headline; a wide interval became a point).
2. **Level shift:** the finding is about X, the circulating version is about Y (results about tasks become claims about people; a population correlation becomes an individual prognosis).
3. **Promotion by label:** anything called a "law", "principle", "effect", "syndrome", "paradox" or "theorem" outside the field where the word is technical. Suspect until shown to exist under that name in the source.
4. **False provenance:** the origin was swapped for a more prestigious one (a famous quote given to a famous person; a concept named after someone who did not formulate it).
5. **Deliberate suppression:** someone removed the information on purpose (posthumous editing, official history).
6. **Filling a material gap:** information lost in the object (ruin, fossil, damaged manuscript) replaced by the aesthetically expected hypothesis.
7. **Numbered scheme with erased authorship:** a list of N items (stages, levels, types, principles) stabilised by convention and given a founder. Suspect until shown in the original.

Patterns 3 and 7 are mechanical sweeps and pay best.

## Reifying forms to hunt

1. "X **is** Y" where the source says "X **works as** Y".
2. Agency given to a model, gene, algorithm, system or abstraction ("the gene wants", "the algorithm decides").
3. A process turned into a thing ("becoming" turned into "the state of").
4. A triad, numbered scheme or acronym the source does not use.
5. A closed definition of a concept the author left open on purpose.
6. An analogy imported from another field without saying where it breaks (every analogy has a negative side).
7. "According to science", "philosophy says", "studies show": fields do not have one opinion.
8. Motivational flattening: a structural, historical or cosmological thesis turned into life advice.

## Marking status without writing like a drug leaflet

The voice rules ban **empty hedging** ("it's important to note", "so to speak"). This file demands **status markers**. The difference:

> **A good caveat adds a fact.** If the marker brings no name, date, authorship or the exact point where the analogy breaks, it is hedging and goes.

- Bad: "In a sense, we might say dialectics is more or less a triad."
- Good: "That formula is not Hegel's. The terms are Fichte's, and they reached the textbooks through a Dresden professor, Chalybäus, in 1837."
- Bad: "The brain is, so to speak, like a computer."
- Good: "The computer comparison holds until the brain stops being digital, synchronous and serial, and turns out to be the opposite of all three. Past that point it misleads."

Ways that work in prose and in speech: name who simplified it and when; date the metaphor (the brain was a hydraulic machine, a telegraph and a switchboard before it was a computer); say where the analogy breaks, once, at the moment it is most comfortable; use the operation verb ("works as", "reads as", "describes the level at which"); show the disagreement as a scene ("translators fought over this for forty years").

Each of these is also **substance** for the voice gate: done well, status marking improves the text.

## Four-level status scale

| Level | What it is | How it may appear |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Anchored** | in the primary source, with exact location | direct statement |
| **2 · Consensus** | majority reading of qualified secondary literature, no relevant dispute | direct statement, naming the commentator when the wording is theirs |
| **3 · Disputed** | documented disagreement between readings | the disagreement is the content; never pick a side silently |
| **4 · Our model** | an analogy, image or scheme we made to explain | must be marked as ours the first time |

A claim that fits none of the four is not content: it is a hypothesis. It goes back to research or becomes an explicit "to verify" note. **A declared gap is a deliverable**, not a failure.

## Question hygiene (before asking any model or search tool)

- Never build the conclusion into the question: not "explain why X is Y" but "what is the status of the relation between X and Y in [author/field], and who disagrees?".
- Ask for a taxonomy, not a verdict: the main readings, with commentator, work and year, and where they disagree.
- Separate layers: what the primary source literally says (or "term absent from the primary work"), then the genealogy of the commentators who popularised the wording.
- Ask for the negative analogy: where the image breaks.
- Ask the tool to refute your hypothesis, and to declare its confidence and whether each claim comes from a primary source, secondary literature or a common pattern with no identifiable source.

The reified answer of a generic model is a good map of common sense (useful as a hook to correct) and useless as a foundation.

## Status gate (checklist)

- [ ] Copula sweep: every central "X is Y" checked against the source (identity, or only function/analogy?).
- [ ] Agency sweep: no model, gene, algorithm or abstraction wanting, seeking or deciding without declared licence.
- [ ] Every three-term formula or numbered scheme exists in the original, or the convention's author is named.
- [ ] Every central analogy has, somewhere, the sentence that says where it breaks.
- [ ] Disagreement shown, not resolved in silence; the conclusion does not quietly settle a dispute the text called open.
- [ ] Central claims traceable to an exact location, or declared as secondary reading.
- [ ] Caveats in the body, next to the claim, not only in a final "limitations" section.
- [ ] Nothing comes from unverified model output.
- [ ] Formulas in LaTeX with their source location; a formula as an image is not auditable.
